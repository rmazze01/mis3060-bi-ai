# HW03 Analysis: Executive Changes vs. Earnings Announcements

Based on my data, executive changes lean toward coming **before** earnings announcements: of the 32 events in `corporate_events_timeline.csv`, 22 were filed before the nearest earnings release, 8 after, and 2 in the same week (both Walmart, 6 days before its Q3 FY26 release). That count overstates the pattern, though, because it counts people rather than filings. Walmart's single January 16, 2026 filing alone produced 6 rows, and 3 of the "before" rows are extraction errors ("Non-Competition Agreements," "Covenant Not," and a duplicate "Nora Johnson"). Counting each of the 19 filings once gives a more modest 11 before, 7 after, and 1 same week. The pattern also varies by company: Walmart, JPMorgan, Apple, and NVIDIA filed most of their changes in the weeks before earnings, while Microsoft went the other way, with 3 of its 4 filings coming after. Because earnings come out roughly every 90 days, every event falls within about 45 days of one, so with only 19 filings over 12 months this looks like a weak tendency rather than a clear rule. A clearer pattern shows up only in the biggest leadership changes (Apple's CEO transition, JPMorgan's June 2026 reshuffle, and Walmart's January 2026 restructuring), which were all announced 10–34 days before the next earnings release, possibly so the new leaders would be in place before the earnings call.
