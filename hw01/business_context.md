2. A summary of Claude's response (3–5 sentences — do not paste the full response)  
(Summary generated by Claude) Delinquency means a borrower has missed a payment but the loan is still active and expected to recover — the lender's focus is on curing it through reminders or hardship plans. Default is a formal declaration that the loan has materially broken down (typically after 90-180+ days unpaid), shifting the lender's focus from recovery-through-repayment to recovery-through-collections, write-off, or legal action. The two carry different credit-reporting impacts, different loss-reserve treatment under accounting rules, and different operational workflows. Lenders keep them separate because delinquency is a leading indicator of stress that might still resolve, while default is closer to a realized loss — merging them into one "problem loan" bucket would erase that distinction and make risk provisioning and portfolio forecasting far less accurate.
